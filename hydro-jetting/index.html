Emergency Hydro Jetting in Mahwah, NJ

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure stream of water to scour the inside of a pipe, removing grease, scale and roots that a cable only punches through. Hydro jetting in Mahwah, NJ is the right call when a main line keeps backing up, a kitchen line is coated with grease or a camera shows buildup narrowing the pipe. The 24/7 line is open, and same-day work is scheduled when equipment is available.

When a sewer backup is an emergency

A slow drain can wait. A backup cannot. Raw sewage in a basement is a health problem and a cleanup problem, and every hour it sits increases the damage to floors and stored belongings. When the toilet bubbles and the floor drain overflows, call the 24/7 line before the situation grows.

  • !Stop running water, including the washer, dishwasher and showers
  • !Keep people and pets away from the affected area and avoid walking through standing water
  • !Do not pour chemical cleaners into a backed-up line
  • !Turn off power to a flooded area only if you can reach the switch without standing in water

How hydro jetting works

  1. Inspect the line with a camera

    A camera shows the pipe material and condition, and whether jetting is safe. This step prevents a jet from damaging a weakened pipe.

  2. Insert the jetting hose through the cleanout

    A hose with a specially shaped nozzle is fed into the line, usually from the main cleanout.

  3. Scour from the pipe wall in

    Water exits the nozzle in rear-facing and angled jets that pull the hose forward and scrub the pipe wall in a full circle.

  4. Flush the debris out

    The loosened grease, scale and roots are carried down the line, and the pass is repeated until the water runs clear.

  5. Camera again

    A follow-up camera pass confirms the pipe is clean and records what is left, such as an offset joint or an old repair.

Where jetting helps in Mahwah homes

Houses with long sewer runs to the street, wooded lots with mature roots and older homes with cast iron or clay laterals all collect more debris over time. Mahwah is the largest municipality in Bergen County by area, and many properties sit well back from the road, which means a longer lateral and more chances for buildup. Jetting resets the pipe to its full diameter, so the line carries flow as designed.

In commercial settings, such as restaurants and shops along Route 17, grease is the main problem, and routine jetting on a schedule keeps kitchens running. For those, see commercial plumbing.

When jetting is not the right tool

  • A pipe that is cracked, collapsed or heavily offset can be damaged by pressure. A camera inspection decides this first.
  • A clog caused by a solid object, such as a toy or a broken fitting, may need to be pulled out rather than flushed.
  • A sag in the pipe, called a belly, will fill with water no matter how clean the line is. That needs repair, not cleaning.

If the camera shows the pipe is damaged, the plan changes to sewer line repair. Jetting is for lines that are structurally sound and just dirty.
