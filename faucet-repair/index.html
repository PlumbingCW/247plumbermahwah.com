When a faucet problem is an emergency

A slow drip is a nuisance. A supply line that has split under a sink, a shower valve that will not close or a tub spout that sprays behind a wall is an emergency, because water is escaping where it can soak cabinets and drywall. Shutting the small valve under a sink usually stops the flood while you call.

Shut off first, then call when

  • !A faucet will not turn off and the shutoff valve below is stuck
  • !A supply line under the sink is dripping or spraying
  • !Water is leaking behind a shower wall or through the ceiling below a bathroom
  • !The handle has come off and the water runs on its own
  • !Water is flowing from a frozen or split outdoor spigot

Calls are answered 24/7, and after-hours faucet emergencies are scheduled the same way as daytime jobs. We tell you plainly when a shutoff and a morning visit are the better plan.

Why faucets drip and leak

Faucet type and typical failure
Faucet typeCommon failureRepair
Compression with two handlesWorn rubber washer or damaged seatReplace washers and dress or replace the seat
Cartridge single handleWorn O-rings or a scale-clogged cartridgeReplace the cartridge and O-rings
Ball-type single handleWorn seats, springs and the ball itselfReplace the seat and spring kit or the ball
Ceramic discCracked disc or worn sealsReplace the disc cylinder
Shower valveWorn cartridge or deteriorated stemsReplace the cartridge. Replace the valve if the body is damaged

Hard water speeds up most of these failures. Mahwah's 2026 water report lists hardness between 91 and 203 ppm, enough to leave scale inside a cartridge and on an aerator. A faucet with low pressure and an intact cartridge may only need its aerator soaked and cleaned.

For a cartridge faucet that drips from the spout, replacing the cartridge is usually faster and longer lasting than repeated tinkering with the old one. Matching the brand and model matters, since cartridges are not interchangeable.

Repair or replace the faucet

Repair when

The faucet is in good condition, parts are available and the problem is a worn cartridge, washer or O-ring.

Replace when

The body is corroded, the finish is failing or parts are no longer made.

Upgrade for

Better flow, touchless operation, a pull-down spray or a style change.

Check the valves

Old angle stops under the sink often fail when turned, so replacing them with the faucet saves a callback.

The shutoff valves under your sink

The small valves beneath a sink or behind a toilet are called angle stops. They rarely get used, and over the years the stem seals can seize or weep. A stop that does not shut fully makes every faucet repair harder, because the plumber must shut the main water instead. Replacing them during a repair is inexpensive insurance against the next emergency.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Safety steps

  • !Close the shutoff valve under the sink, or the main valve if the fixture has none.
  • !Do not force a handle that will not turn, since a cracked valve can flood the cabinet.
  • !Dry the area under the sink and place a bucket under any steady drip.
  • !Turn off power to nearby outlets if water has reached them.
  • !Keep the old parts, which help the plumber match the replacement.

Codes and standards that apply

Faucet parts that touch drinking water should carry NSF/ANSI 61 marking, which is the standard for materials in contact with drinking water. New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Check the packaging before you buy a part or a new faucet.
