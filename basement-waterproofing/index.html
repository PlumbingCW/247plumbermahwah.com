Basement Waterproofing in Mahwah, NJ

A basement that is damp after every rain is telling you where the water goes. Basement waterproofing in Mahwah, NJ means finding the source of the moisture and managing it, usually with an interior drain channel along the footing, a sump pit and pump, better grading and gutters, and sealing where water enters. The 24/7 line is open, and same-day assessments are offered when available.

Is it a leak or groundwater?

Water in a basement comes from three places: the plumbing, the surface and the ground. A plumbing leak shows up regardless of weather. Surface water comes from poor grading and gutters. Groundwater rises through the floor or the wall base after rain. The fix for each is different, and treating the wrong one wastes money.

The Ramapo Valley water table

Mahwah sits in a valley where the Ramapo River drains the surrounding hills, and the river valley has glacial sand and gravel that holds groundwater. The township's own wellhead protection ordinance describes the glacial aquifers in the Ramapo River Valley. That same gravel lets water move quickly, so heavy rain can raise the water table around a basement without a river flood.

Interior drainage and sump systems

  1. Open a channel along the footing

    A strip of floor slab is cut along the wall, and a drain channel is set in gravel.

  2. Slope the channel to a sump pit

    The channel runs to a pit installed in the floor, where a pump removes the water.

  3. Install the pump and discharge

    A submersible pump with a check valve sends water outdoors, away from the house.

  4. Add a backup

    A battery backup keeps the system working during outages.

  5. Restore the floor

    The slab is patched, and the wall base is sealed so water goes to the channel and not across the floor.

What you can do before any contractor

  • Extend downspouts several feet away from the foundation so roof water does not soak the base of the wall.
  • Clean gutters and check that they are not overflowing at the corners.
  • Grade soil to slope away from the house, with the top few inches of soil sloping at least a few inches over several feet.
  • Check window wells for debris and drainage.
  • Keep a record of when water appears and how much rain fell, since that pattern tells the plumber the source.

What we do not do

We handle plumbing-based drainage, which includes interior drains, sump pits and pumps, discharge lines and leaks. Structural foundation repair, exterior excavation and waterproofing membranes belong to other trades. We tell you when a wall crack or settlement needs a structural contractor so you get the right help.

Basement painting is a separate question, since paint cannot stop hydrostatic pressure. Waterproof paint can slow moisture on a dry slab, but it does not replace drainage.
