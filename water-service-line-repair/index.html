When a service line problem is an emergency

A broken service line can send a steady stream of water into the soil with nothing showing indoors, or it can cut the supply to the house entirely. Either way, you are paying for water that goes nowhere or living without it. The line is under pressure from the street, so the loss does not stop until the valve is closed.

Signs of a failing service line

  • !A wet, soft or sunken patch in the yard that stays wet in dry weather
  • !Sudden loss of water pressure at every tap
  • !No water at all, or air and sputtering from faucets
  • !A water bill that jumps with no change in use
  • !The meter spinning when all fixtures are off

Call the 24/7 line anytime. After-hours and same-day service is scheduled when a crew is available, and we tell you plainly about any wait, so you know when to arrange for water in the meantime.

Who owns what

In most systems, the water utility owns the main in the street and the connection up to the shutoff at the curb or the meter, and the homeowner owns the pipe from there to the house. The exact boundary differs, so ask your utility what applies at your address. Mahwah runs its own water system, and the township's Water and Sewer office can tell you which side of the boundary your leak is on.

What the service line is made of

Service lines in the area may be copper, plastic or older materials such as galvanized steel or lead, depending on when the house was connected. Ask your water utility what material your service line is made of. Mahwah's water report notes that the township adds a phosphate compound to reduce lead release in household plumbing.

The report lists lead at 4.38 ppb at the 90th percentile in 2024 sampling, under the 15 ppb action level. If you have an old service line, replacement is a way to remove one source of risk and a frequent failure point.

Repair or replace

Spot repair

Best when a single break is found and the rest of the pipe is sound.

Full replacement

Better for old, corroded or lead lines and for lines that have failed more than once.

Trenchless methods

Pipe bursting or pulling a new line through a small bore, where soil and layout allow, reducing yard damage.

Traditional trench

The most direct way to replace the line, with full access to the pipe and the soil.

  1. Locate the line and the leak

    Acoustic listening and a line locator mark the path and the problem.

  2. Check utilities and permits

    Buried utilities are marked before any digging. Permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office what your job needs.

  3. Expose and repair or replace

    The line is exposed at the break or along its length and replaced with new pipe rated for potable water.

  4. Pressure test and flush

    The new pipe is tested and flushed before water is returned to the house.

  5. Restore

    Soil is backfilled and the surface restored as agreed in the scope.

What affects the price of a water service line repair

Service line work varies with length, depth and what is above the pipe. The plumber should show the scope and price before digging.
