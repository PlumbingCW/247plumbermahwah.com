What your home's age says about its plumbing

Allendale was incorporated in 1894 and covers 3.12 square miles, with 2,450 housing units counted in 2020. That mix includes homes built over more than a century, and we do not assume what any one house contains. The age of the house is the first clue to the pipe material, and the table below shows what to look for.

House age, likely pipes and what to check

If you do not know your home's age, a plumber can identify the pipes in a short visit. That is also the way to find out whether a sewer camera inspection makes sense before you buy or renovate.

Where Allendale sits

Allendale borders Mahwah, Ramsey, Saddle River, Waldwick and Wyckoff, and Route 17 touches its far eastern edge. The NJ Transit Allendale station serves the Main Line and Bergen County Line. Crestwood Park covers 71 acres and the Celery Farm nature preserve sits in the borough. For homeowners near the lake and park, groundwater can run higher after storms, so a working sump pump is worth a test each spring. See sump pump service.

Water in Allendale

The borough's water page says Allendale draws from five wells, numbered 11, 17, 2, 4 and 15, and lists a bulk purchase agreement as an additional source. It does not state a hardness figure, so we do not quote one. Ask the borough for its annual water quality report, or look at your own fixtures for scale.

If your heater rumbles or your showerhead clogs with white deposits, the water is leaving minerals behind. A yearly flush of the tank, or a descale of a tankless unit, is the fix. See tankless water heaters and water heater repair.
