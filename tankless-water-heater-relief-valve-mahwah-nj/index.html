Tankless Water Heater Relief Valve for Mahwah, NJ Homes

A relief valve is a safety device that opens to release water if pressure or temperature goes too high. Whether a tankless water heater needs one depends on the manufacturer and the installation, and a dripping valve is a warning that should not be ignored.

If a relief valve is releasing steam or hot water steadily, turn off the heater and keep people away from the discharge pipe, then call.

What a relief valve does

A temperature and pressure relief valve, often called a T&P valve, is a spring-loaded valve that opens when water pressure or temperature passes a set limit. It prevents a vessel from building up dangerous pressure. On a tank heater it is standard. Tankless heaters hold very little water, so the manufacturer's instructions decide whether one is required.

Many tankless installations still include a pressure relief valve on the hot water side, particularly where the house has a closed plumbing system, a recirculation loop or a storage tank in the system. Always follow the manual for your model and the local code official.

Why a relief valve drips

A valve that drips is doing its job or has stopped doing it. Neither is a reason to cap it. Capping a relief valve defeats the safety purpose and creates a risk of rupture.

Closed systems and expansion tanks

When water heats, it expands. In an open system the extra water pushes back into the street main. If a backflow preventer or a pressure reducing valve is on the house supply, the water cannot go back, and pressure rises. The relief valve opens to protect the system, and an expansion tank is the proper fix because it gives the extra water somewhere to go.

Check the pressure

A simple gauge on a hose bib shows household pressure. Many homes should sit within a moderate range.

Check the tank

If an expansion tank is present, its air charge should match the house pressure.

Check the discharge pipe

It must run safely to a floor drain or the outdoors, and never be capped.

Test the valve

Lift the lever briefly once a year, with a bucket under the discharge, to confirm it moves freely.

What a plumber checks

Relief valve inspection

  • ✓The valve rating matches the manufacturer's specification
  • ✓The discharge pipe is the right size, slopes down and ends in a visible place
  • ✓House pressure is within range, with a pressure reducing valve if needed
  • ✓An expansion tank is present and charged where the system is closed
  • ✓The heater's temperature setting and any scale buildup in the heat exchanger

For gas tankless units the venting and gas supply are checked at the same visit. See tankless water heater service for installation, repair and flushing, and water heater repair for related problems.

Reading the valve label

Every relief valve carries a label with its set pressure and its set temperature. Temperature and pressure valves for water heaters are commonly set at 150 pounds per square inch and 210 degrees Fahrenheit, though the right rating depends on the heater and its working pressure. The valve must never be rated above the heater's own limit.

If you are replacing a valve, bring the old one to the supply house and match the rating, the thread size and the length of the probe if it has one. A valve rated too low drips constantly. One rated too high may not open when it should.

Do not reuse a valve that has been dripping for years. The spring and seat wear, and a valve that has opened often is the first one to stick.

If the valve drips only during heating, ask whether the system has an expansion tank or a pressure problem. If it drips constantly, the valve itself may be worn. Do not plug the discharge pipe, and do not replace the valve with a different type. A plumber can read the pressure with a gauge and tell you which problem you have.

Mahwah code and water notes

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code, according to N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Permit rules vary, so ask the Mahwah construction office before work that changes venting, gas piping or relief discharge.

Mahwah's 2026 water quality report lists hardness from 91 to 203 ppm. Scale can bake onto a relief valve seat and keep it from sealing, so a dripping valve in a hard water area is often a worn valve with deposits and not a pressure problem.
