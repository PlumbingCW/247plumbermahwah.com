About Fardale

Mahwah Township lists Fardale and Masonicus together as former farming and residential areas that now hold many single-family homes and condominium developments. Fardale does not have official boundaries on the township page, so we describe it by its roads. The Mahwah Museum names Fardale Avenue, Chapel Road, Pulis Avenue, Campgaw Road, Youngs Road and Bartholf Lane.

From farms to subdivisions

The museum describes a commercial farming district. Families named Bartholf, Bogert, Winter, Van Gelder, Ackerman and Young held large tracts, and farms grew vegetables, blueberries and poultry. Produce went by rail to the depot in Ramsey, and the farmland was subdivided repeatedly over time.

Farming in Fardale effectively ended in the mid-1970s. Two developments the museum names are Glenmere Park, begun in 1965 on former farmland, and Chapel Greens, a condominium project on the old Bartholf farm. The result is a section with housing from several periods side by side.

House or condominium: who fixes what

In a single-family house, you generally own and maintain the plumbing from the street connection through the house. In a condominium, the split between the association and the unit owner depends on the governing documents. A leak inside your wall can be your responsibility or the association's, depending on where it is.

  • Read the declaration or ask the management office which pipes the association maintains.
  • Find your unit's water shutoff before you need it.
  • Tell the management office quickly if water could reach a unit below.
  • Take dated photos of damage before cleanup, for the association and your insurer.

This is general information about how condominiums work, not a claim about any particular Fardale community. A plumber can tell you what is inside your unit, and the association can tell you what it covers.

Water in Fardale

The township water report describes the municipal system as a whole, with seven wells in the Ramapo River valley aquifer supplying 67.2 percent and Veolia supplying 32.8 percent. We have not confirmed which supply serves each Fardale street, so ask the Water and Sewer office about yours.

The same report gives a hardness range of 91 to 203 parts per million for the township, which the USGS scale reads as moderately hard at the low end and very hard at the top. Ask the Water and Sewer office which end of that range your tap sits near, since the report does not say street by street.

Nearby

Fardale shares the township with other sections such as Cragmere Park and West Mahwah. If you are closer to the Ramsey line, the Ramsey plumbing page may also help.

Codes and permits

Plumbing work in New Jersey follows the National Standard Plumbing Code under N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. For permits, ask the local construction office, and ask your condominium association whether it requires notice of work.
