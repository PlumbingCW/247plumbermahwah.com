What a good installation includes

Any heater can be set in a corner and plumbed. A good installation also gets the details around it right: a relief valve with a safe discharge, correct venting, an expansion tank where the system is closed, shutoff valves within reach and clear access for service. Those items are the difference between a heater that runs quietly and one that causes a callback.

Items that belong in every installation

  • ✓Shutoff valves on the cold supply and the hot outlet
  • ✓A relief valve with a discharge pipe that stops short of the floor or runs to a drain
  • ✓An expansion tank on a closed system, sized and charged for the house pressure
  • ✓Correct venting and combustion air for gas units
  • ✓A dedicated circuit and correctly sized wiring for electric units
  • ✓A drain pan and drain line when the heater is above a finished space

Sizing without guesswork

Count how many people live in the house, how many bathrooms they use in the morning and whether the dishwasher or washing machine runs while someone showers. For a tank, the first-hour rating tells you how much hot water the unit can deliver in a busy hour. For a tankless heater, the flow rate and temperature rise decide capacity.

Incoming water temperature matters in New Jersey winters, because cold supply water makes a tankless unit work harder. A plumber who sizes the unit for January rather than July avoids the mid-shower surprise.

Gas, venting and the Mahwah housing mix

Mahwah covers a lot of ground. Its homes include older houses in Cragmere Park and newer developments built since, so existing venting and gas piping vary widely. A flue that served a 40-gallon atmospheric tank may not suit a condensing unit, and gas lines sized for a stove and a furnace may not carry a tankless heater without an upgrade.

New Jersey's plumbing subcode is based on the National Standard Plumbing Code, and permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office before work that changes fuel, venting or gas piping. The plumber can tell you what your installation needs.

The installation, step by step

  1. Site visit or phone survey

    Location, fuel, vent path, panel capacity and access are noted so the unit and parts are right the first time.

  2. Rough-in

    Gas, water and venting are positioned, and any electrical circuit is installed by a qualified person.

  3. Set and connect

    The heater is set level, connected with new flexible lines and fitted with a relief valve and discharge pipe.

  4. Fill, purge and test

    The tank is filled and air is purged before the burner or element is switched on.

  5. Inspection items

    Gas connections are leak tested, combustion is checked, and the owner is shown the shutoffs.
