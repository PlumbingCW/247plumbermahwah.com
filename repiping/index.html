Repiping in Mahwah, NJ

Repiping replaces a home's supply pipes when repairs no longer hold. Repiping in Mahwah, NJ is considered when a house has galvanized steel that has rusted shut, polybutylene that is failing, copper with repeated pinhole leaks or water that comes out brown and weak. The 24/7 line is open for scheduling, and same-day inspections are offered when available.

If you have an active leak now, close the main shutoff and call. A repiping decision can wait, but water damage cannot.

Signs your house may need repiping

A single leak is a repair. A pattern of leaks is a warning. If a pipe fails in one place and another fails within a year or two, the whole run may be at the end of its life. Pressure that falls when two taps run together and water that comes out brown or rusty are other clues.

Common signs

  • ✓Repeated pinhole leaks in copper or leaks at galvanized fittings
  • ✓Low water pressure that has worsened over the years
  • ✓Brown or orange water that clears slowly
  • ✓Pipes with visible green or white buildup, flaking or bulges
  • ✓Polybutylene or galvanized pipe identified during a purchase inspection

Pipe materials by house age

The materials in your walls depend on when the house was built and what has been replaced since. Check the age and then confirm with a plumber, because earlier owners may have swapped sections over the years.

Mahwah water and your pipes

The Mahwah water report for 2026 lists pH as high as 8.2 and notes that the township adds a phosphate compound that forms a protective coating inside pipes to reduce lead release. That treatment is aimed at the water system and the pipes it feeds. It does not repair a pipe that is already thinned by corrosion.

Hardness between 91 and 203 ppm means scale builds up inside hot water lines faster than in soft water areas, so a repipe is a good time to add isolation valves and consider a whole-house filter or conditioner.

Copper, PEX or CPVC

Copper

Long track record, tolerates heat and does not break down in sunlight. It is the most expensive and takes the most labor.

PEX

Flexible, fast to install and resistant to freeze splitting. Needs correct fittings and ultraviolet protection.

CPVC

Rigid plastic, inexpensive and corrosion resistant. Can crack with age.

Your decision

A plumber can explain which fits your walls, budget and building code.

What a repipe involves

  1. Survey

    The plumber traces the existing lines, counts fixtures and decides where walls and ceilings will be opened.

  2. Plan and permit

    The route and the materials are chosen. Permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office what your job requires.

  3. Install new lines

    New supply pipes are run, usually through walls, basements and attics, with isolation valves at key points.

  4. Pressure test

    The new system is pressure tested before any wall is closed.

  5. Restore and flush

    Access holes are patched and the system is flushed before it is returned to service.

What affects the price of repiping

Homeowners insurance typically does not pay to replace pipes that are worn out. It may cover sudden water damage from a pipe that bursts, so prompt repairs matter.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Safety steps

Frequently asked questions

Galvanized pipe is gray metal that attracts a magnet. Polybutylene is flexible gray, blue or black plastic with markings like PB2110. A plumber can identify the material during a visit.

A typical whole-house repipe takes days, depending on size and access. A partial repipe of one branch can be done faster. The plumber gives you a schedule when the job is planned.
