When a boiler problem needs a call right away

A cold house in January is hard on people and pipes. Once indoor temperatures drop, pipes in exterior walls can freeze and split, turning a heating problem into a flooding one. A boiler that leaks, makes loud banging noises or shows a safety lockout deserves attention soon.

Safety first

  • !Gas smell: leave the house and call your gas utility or 911
  • !Carbon monoxide alarm: get everyone outside and call 911
  • !Water leaking from the boiler: shut off the water feed valve and the power switch
  • !Pressure gauge above the red zone or the relief valve discharging: turn the boiler off and call
  • !No heat in freezing weather: open cabinet doors on exterior walls and keep a trickle at vulnerable taps

How a hydronic system works

A hydronic boiler heats water and a circulator pump sends it through pipes to radiators, baseboards or in-floor loops. Zone valves open and close to heat different parts of the house, and an expansion tank gives the heated water somewhere to go. When one of those parts fails, the symptoms look similar, which is why diagnosis comes before parts.

Circulator pump

Moves the hot water. A failed pump leaves the boiler hot while rooms stay cold.

Zone valve

Opens when a thermostat calls for heat. A stuck valve starves one zone.

Expansion tank

Absorbs pressure as water heats. A waterlogged tank causes pressure swings.

Relief valve

Opens to protect the boiler from over-pressure. A dripping valve is a warning.

Before the cold weather

  • Have the boiler serviced before the heating season, with a combustion check on gas models.
  • Bleed air from radiators and check the pressure gauge. Most residential boilers run in a modest range shown on the gauge.
  • Keep the area around the boiler clear for air and access.
  • Test carbon monoxide alarms and replace batteries.

What affects the price of boiler repair

A thermostat issue and a failed heat exchanger are very different. The plumber should diagnose first and give you a price before parts are ordered.

The failed partA relief valve or a zone valve costs far less than a control board or a heat exchanger.
Boiler ageOlder boilers may need parts that are hard to find.
Fuel and typeGas and electric systems differ in components and testing.
System designMultiple zones and radiant loops add complexity.
Time of the callAfter-hours visits can carry a different rate, stated first.

Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden water damage from a boiler leak but not the boiler itself. Frozen pipe damage from a heating failure may be covered, but check your policy.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Safety steps

  • !If you smell gas, leave the house, then call the gas utility from outside. Do not use switches or flames.
  • !If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, go outside into fresh air and call 911.
  • !Do not relight a pilot that keeps going out, and do not bypass a safety control.
  • !If water is leaking from the boiler, shut off the water feed and turn off the power at the switch.
  • !Keep children and pets away from hot pipes and the boiler jacket.

Codes and standards that apply

Boiler work follows the manufacturer's installation standards and the plumbing and fuel gas rules that apply in your town. New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15 for the piping side. Permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office before a boiler is replaced or its venting is changed.
