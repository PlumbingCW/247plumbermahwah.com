Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer line that has failed lets wastewater leak into the soil or back up into the house. Either way it is a health issue as well as a plumbing one. When more than one fixture drains slowly or a floor drain overflows, the line may have a break, a collapse or a blockage that keeps returning.

Sewer emergency checklist

  • !Stop running water and do not flush toilets
  • !Keep children and pets away from standing water and wipe nothing with bare hands
  • !Do not use chemical cleaners. They can damage the line and burn skin
  • !Open windows if sewer gas is noticeable and avoid ignition sources
  • !Call the 24/7 line and describe which fixtures are affected

Repair options explained

Spot repair

Digging down to a single bad section and replacing it. Good when the camera shows one problem and the rest of the pipe is sound.

Pipe lining

A resin-saturated liner is pulled through the pipe and cured in place, creating a new pipe inside the old one. Less digging, but needs a pipe that is mostly intact.

Pipe bursting

A new pipe is pulled through as the old one is broken apart. Fits when the old line is beyond lining and access is reasonable.

Full replacement

Trenching and installing new pipe the full length. Most disruptive but resets the line.

The right choice depends on the camera findings, the pipe material, the depth, what is above it and the total length. A plumber who recommends the most expensive option without showing you the footage should be questioned.

Mahwah sewer lines and older homes

Houses built decades ago may have cast iron, clay or fiber pipe, while newer homes use PVC. Older materials are more prone to root intrusion and corrosion. Mahwah has wooded lots with mature trees, and roots are a frequent cause of failure. Confirm whether your property connects to the township sewer or to a septic system, because that changes where the line goes and who is responsible. We work on the house-side line and do not pump septic tanks.

Mahwah Township's Department of Public Works runs the Water and Sewer office. Ask it who maintains the part of the line beyond your property line before digging at the street, since responsibility is split between the owner and the municipality in most systems.

Codes and standards that apply

Sewer repairs must meet the plumbing code, and New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Replacing a lateral often needs a permit and an inspection, and the rules differ by town, so ask the local construction office. Ask the town who is responsible for which section of the lateral, since the boundary differs by municipality.
