When a disposal problem is an emergency

A jammed disposal is a nuisance. A disposal that is leaking under the sink, sparking at the switch or backing sink water into the dishwasher is a hazard. Water and electricity share a small cabinet, and a leak can run to the floor of the cabinet and beyond within minutes.

Safety first

  • !Switch off the disposal and its breaker before any inspection
  • !Never put a hand into the chamber. Use tongs or pliers for a visible object only after power is off
  • !If water is leaking, close the supply valves for the sink and the dishwasher
  • !If you smell burning or see sparks, shut off the breaker and call
  • !Keep the sink empty until the line is clear

What should not go into a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat. They cool and coat the drain line even if they flow through the disposal.
  • Coffee grounds in volume, eggshells and pasta or rice, which swell and pack the line.
  • Fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.
  • Bones, pits and hard shells. A disposal grinds soft food and is not a crusher.

Mahwah's hard water also leaves mineral scale on grinding surfaces and in the drain line, so a disposal here is better flushed with plenty of cold water while running and then for a minute after.

Replacing or installing a disposal

  1. Choose the unit

    Horsepower and feed type matter. Continuous-feed disposals are common, and batch-feed models run only with the stopper in place.

  2. Check the electrical

    A switched outlet under the sink or a hardwired connection is needed, and a qualified person handles any new wiring.

  3. Mount and connect

    The sink flange, mounting ring and disposal are assembled, then the drain trap and the dishwasher hose are connected.

  4. Remove the knockout plug

    If a dishwasher connects, the plug inside the disposal inlet has to be removed. Missed plugs cause dishwasher backups.

  5. Test it

    The unit is run with water and the connections are checked for leaks.

Codes and standards that apply

Disposal connections follow the standards in the plumbing code, which New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15 for traps, air gaps and drain connections. Check the manufacturer's manual for the model's electrical and mounting requirements. Permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office if you are adding a new circuit.
