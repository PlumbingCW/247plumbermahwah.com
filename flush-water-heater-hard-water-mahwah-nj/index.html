How to Flush a Water Heater in Hard Water: Mahwah, NJ Guide

Flushing a water heater drains the sediment that settles on the bottom of the tank. In hard water, that sediment builds faster, makes the heater work harder and shortens its life. This guide shows how to flush a tank safely and how often to do it.

If the heater is leaking or you smell gas, do not flush. Shut off the supply and call.

Why flushing matters

Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. When water is heated, those minerals come out of solution and form scale and sediment. On a gas tank, sediment sits between the burner and the water. On an electric tank, it coats the lower element. Both make the heater less efficient and add noise.

The rumbling or popping that many owners hear is water trapped under the sediment boiling off. A flush removes most of it. A tank that has never been flushed may have a drain valve clogged with scale, which is a repair job on its own.

What the hardness numbers mean

ClassHardness as calcium carbonate
Soft0 to 60 mg/L

Very hardMore than 180 mg/L

One milligram per liter is the same as one part per million, so the ppm figures in a water quality report fit this scale directly.

Flush a tank water heater

  1. Turn off the power or gas

    Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas control to pilot or off.

  2. Close the cold supply valve

    This stops new water from entering the tank.

  3. Open a hot tap

    Open a hot water faucet in the house so air can enter and the tank can drain.

  4. Connect a hose to the drain valve

    Run it to a floor drain or outdoors, since the water will be hot.

  5. Open the drain valve

    Let the tank drain. Expect cloudy water and sediment.

  6. Briefly open the supply valve

    With the drain open, pulse cold water in to stir sediment until the water runs clear.

  7. Close, refill and restart

    Close the drain, refill the tank with the hot tap open until water flows steadily, then restore power or gas.

Safety cautions

  • ✓The water is hot enough to scald. Let the tank rest a few hours or run the hose to a safe location
  • ✓Never turn on an electric heater while the tank is empty
  • ✓If a gas pilot goes out, follow the label instructions or call a plumber
  • ✓If the drain valve is plastic and brittle, do not force it. A plumber can replace it

How often to flush

In soft water, every year or two may be enough. In hard or very hard water, once a year is a good habit. A heater that rumbles or runs out of hot water quickly may be due now. See water heater repair if flushing does not help, and water heater replacement if the tank is old and failing.

Tankless heaters need descaling

Tankless units cannot be flushed like a tank. Their heat exchangers need descaling with a circulating solution on the schedule in the manual. See tankless water heater service.

Check the anode rod and the relief valve while you are at it

The anode rod is a metal rod inside the tank that corrodes in place of the tank walls. When it is used up, the tank starts to rust. Many rods can be inspected with a socket wrench from the top of the heater. A rod that is thin, bent or mostly bare steel is due for replacement.

Lift the lever on the relief valve briefly with a bucket under the discharge pipe. Water should flow and stop when you let go. If it keeps dripping, the valve needs replacing. A plumber can check both items in the same visit as a flush.

Plan the flush for a time when nobody needs hot water for a few hours. The tank takes a while to drain and refill, and the water coming out of the hose can scald. Let the heater cool with the power off or the gas set to pilot before you start, and route the hose to a floor drain or outdoors where hot water and sediment will not damage anything.

Codes and standards that apply

Follow the manufacturer's maintenance guidelines for flushing, since each model lists its own procedure. Water hardness in Mahwah is measured on the USGS scale, where 121 to 180 ppm is hard and above 180 is very hard. New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.

Mahwah hardness notes

Mahwah's 2026 water quality report lists hardness between 91 and 203 ppm as calcium carbonate, with a calcium level of 52 ppm in the township's groundwater. On the USGS scale that range runs from moderately hard to very hard, which is why Mahwah homeowners often notice scale.

About two thirds of the township's water comes from seven wells along the Ramapo River valley and the rest from Veolia, so hardness can differ between neighborhoods. Look at your showerhead and faucet aerators. White scale on them tells you what the heater is seeing.
