When a toilet problem is an emergency

A single clogged toilet is not usually an emergency. An overflowing one is, and so is a toilet that leaks sewage-tinged water or one that has stopped draining while other fixtures gurgle. Any of those can damage floors and subfloors quickly, and the last one points to a main line problem instead of a toilet problem.

Act fast if you see this

  • !The bowl is rising toward the rim and will not flush down
  • !Water is spreading from the base of the toilet onto the floor
  • !Other fixtures bubble or back up when the toilet is flushed
  • !The tank keeps refilling and the overflow tube is spilling into the bowl
  • !A sewer smell comes from the bathroom floor

Our line answers 24/7, including weekends and holidays. For a toilet that is flooding a floor, shut off the supply valve first and then call us, and a plumber will be scheduled the same day when possible.

Clogged toilet: what works

A flange-style plunger, one with a rubber extension at the bottom, seals into the toilet outlet and moves water through the trap. A standard flat sink plunger does not seal well. Push gently first, then pump with a steady rhythm, and give the water time to drain before trying again.

If a plunger fails, a closet auger reaches the bend of the trap without scratching the porcelain. When multiple toilets and tubs clog together, the problem is below the fixtures, and that is a job for the main-line tools described under drain cleaning.

Replacing a toilet

Replace the toilet when the porcelain is cracked, the toilet clogs even when the line is clear or the model uses far more water per flush than current units. A modern toilet saves water and often improves flushing. Installation includes shutting off the water, removing the old toilet, inspecting the flange, setting a new wax ring or seal and testing the flush.

Hard water and toilet parts

Mahwah's 2026 water quality report shows hardness between 91 and 203 ppm. Hard water leaves mineral deposits that clog rim jets and stiffen flappers and fill valve seals. A toilet that has been in service for years may run intermittently because scale has roughened the flapper seat, which is a fast and inexpensive fix.
