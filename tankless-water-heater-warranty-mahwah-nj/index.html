Tankless Water Heater Warranty: Mahwah, NJ Homeowner Guide

A tankless water heater warranty is a bundle of separate promises. The heat exchanger, the other parts and the labor each have different terms, and the maintenance you did often decides whether any of them holds. This guide shows how to read one before you buy and what to keep afterward.

We do not quote a specific manufacturer's terms here, since they change. What follows is a framework you can use against any warranty sheet.

The three parts of a warranty

Most tankless warranties split into the heat exchanger, other components and labor. They run for different lengths. The heat exchanger usually carries the longest term, other parts a shorter one and labor the shortest or none at all.

What each part of a warranty usually covers
PartWhat it coversWhat to check
Heat exchangerFailure of the core component that heats the waterTerm length, residential or commercial use, and any reduced coverage in later years
PartsControl board, sensors, fans, igniters, valvesTerm length and whether the part is repaired or replaced
LaborThe cost of the technician to replace the partWhether it is covered at all, and for how long
Shipping and removalMoving the part and disposing of the old oneOften excluded

What voids or reduces coverage

Warranties usually exclude damage from improper installation, water quality problems, freezing, lack of required maintenance and use of the heater outside its design. Hard water is an important one. If scale damages the heat exchanger and you have not descaled it on schedule, the manufacturer may deny the claim.

  • Installation by someone who is not qualified, or without the required venting and gas sizing.
  • Skipping the maintenance the manual requires, such as an annual flush in hard water.
  • Operating the heater with water outside the manufacturer's limits for hardness or pH.
  • Damage from freezing, flooding or a power surge.
  • A heater moved to a new owner without the transfer steps the warranty requires.

Keep the paper trail

Records worth keeping

  • ✓The sales receipt and the installer invoice, with the model and serial number
  • ✓The warranty registration confirmation, if the manufacturer requires registration
  • ✓The date, name and notes of every flush or service visit
  • ✓Photos of the installation, including the venting and the gas piping
  • ✓The permit and inspection record, where one applies

If you ever need to file a claim, those records are the evidence. A plumber who services the heater should record the date, what was done and the water condition.

Questions to ask before you buy

How long is each term?

Ask for the heat exchanger, parts and labor terms in writing.

Is registration required?

Some warranties start shorter if you do not register.

What maintenance is required?

Ask how often and who may perform it.

What about hard water?

Ask whether a softener or filter is required for the full warranty.

See our page on tankless water heater service for installation and descaling, and water heater installation for planning. A plumber can read the warranty with you before you commit.

Extended warranties and service plans

Dealers and installers sometimes offer an extended plan on top of the manufacturer's warranty. Some are worth having and some are not. The question is what the plan covers that the manufacturer does not, and who honors the claim.

  • Check whether the plan covers labor, parts or both, and whether there is a deductible for each visit.
  • Ask who performs the repairs, and whether the plan is backed by the installer or by a separate company.
  • Check whether routine descaling is included, since that is the maintenance the manufacturer requires.
  • Read the cancellation and transfer terms before you pay.

A plan that includes the yearly service the manual requires can pay for itself in hard water. A plan that only covers parts the manufacturer already covers adds little.

Keep every record. Save the receipt, the model and serial numbers, the installation date and each service invoice in one folder. If a claim is ever denied for missing maintenance, a dated descaling invoice is the proof that settles it. Photograph the rating label on the unit the day it is installed, since labels fade and can be hard to read behind the cover later.

Read the exclusions line by line. Many warranties void coverage for scale damage, incorrect venting, a missing water filter or an unlicensed installer. If your home has hard water, ask the installer in writing how the unit is protected from scale, and keep that note with your warranty papers.

Mahwah water and warranty terms

Mahwah's 2026 water quality report lists hardness from 91 to 203 ppm as calcium carbonate. Moderately hard to very hard water is exactly the condition where manufacturers ask for annual descaling, so treat the maintenance clause as a real requirement in Mahwah.

New Jersey law says plumbing may only be performed by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or their employees, according to the Division of Consumer Affairs. Use a licensed plumber for installation and service, because a warranty may require professional installation and the work needs to be done correctly.
