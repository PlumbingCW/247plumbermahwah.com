Rough-in: where the quality is decided

Rough-in is the part of the work that ends up behind drywall. Supply lines, drain lines and vents are set at the right height and slope before anyone closes the wall. A drain with too little slope clogs, a vent in the wrong place gurgles and a supply line bent through a stud bay can fail. These errors do not show until the room is finished, which is why rough-in matters.

What a correct rough-in includes

  • ✓Drain lines at the proper slope toward the main stack
  • ✓Vents that connect fixtures to the roof or an air admittance valve where code permits
  • ✓Supply lines protected where they pass through studs and joists
  • ✓Isolation valves for each fixture or zone
  • ✓A pressure test before the wall is covered
  • ✓An inspection by the local code official where required

Codes and permits

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. Permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office what your project requires before work starts. A plumber who works in the area knows the typical steps and can coordinate the inspection.

Basement bathrooms below the sewer line deserve special care. They usually need a sewage ejector pump, a sealed basin and a vent. If the house has a high water table, which is possible in a river valley, a sump pump and the ejector pump should not share a basin. See sump pump installation and repair for more.

Mahwah homes and plumbing projects

Mahwah houses include older homes where the existing plumbing may be galvanized or cast iron, and newer homes with copper or PEX. Connecting new plumbing to old pipe works best after checking the existing material and pressure. A repipe may be part of a larger remodel, and repiping explains when that makes sense.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Safety steps

  • !Locate the main water shutoff and the gas shutoff, and make sure you can reach them.
  • !Clear access to the work area, including the space under sinks and around the heater.
  • !Do not cut into a wall to move a pipe before the plumber sees it.
  • !Ask the plumber about permits and inspections before work starts, since the rules vary by town.
