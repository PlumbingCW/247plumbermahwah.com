Plumbing problems we see in Ramsey

Ramsey is a compact borough of 5.57 square miles and about 14,800 residents at the 2020 census, with a walkable downtown and a lot of established streets. Established streets mean a mix of pipe ages, and the calls follow the pattern. The most common are drains that back up, water heaters that give out in cold weather and supply lines that fail where old fittings meet newer pipe.

Emergency plumbing in Ramsey, step by step

When the problem is urgent

  • !Water or sewage is rising from a floor drain, tub or toilet
  • !A pipe is spraying or a ceiling is sagging with water
  • !A water heater tank is leaking on a finished floor
  • !There is no water at all, or the sump pit is filling with no pump running

Shut off the main water valve for a leak, stop using drains for a backup and call. We tell you what to do while you wait and what the visit will involve.

Your water, your pipes

Ramsey's water is supplied by the borough. The New Jersey Infrastructure Bank reports that the Arrow Road Treatment Facility draws water from three wells and that the borough closed on a loan of 3.25 million dollars from the New Jersey Water Bank for PFAS treatment there. That is a water quality project and it does not change your pipes, but it shows the borough is investing in the system.

We do not quote a hardness figure for Ramsey, because we have not seen one from the borough. Test your tap or ask the Ramsey water department for its annual water quality report. Signs of hard water in your own home, such as scale on fixtures and rumbling in the water heater, are the practical test.

Downtown, Route 17 and the rail corridor

Ramsey has two NJ Transit stations on the Main Line and Bergen County Line. The Main Street station, built in 1868, is the oldest operating passenger station in New Jersey, and the Route 17 station is a park-and-ride. Route 17 and County Route 507 cross the area east and north of downtown. For businesses along those roads, restaurants and shops need quick answers when a line backs up, and our commercial plumbing service covers after-hours calls.

Residential streets near Finch Park, the municipal pool on East Oak Street and Suraci Pond and Garrison Pond are mostly established neighborhoods with mature trees. Roots and old laterals are the usual story there, and a sewer camera inspection shows what the line looks like before it fails.
