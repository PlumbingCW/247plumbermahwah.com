When a sump pump failure is an emergency

A sump pump is quiet until the day it matters. When a storm pushes groundwater into the basement, a pump that fails means water on the floor within an hour or two. The failure often comes with the storm itself, because power flickers and the pump has to work harder at exactly the wrong time.

  • !Lift the float by hand. If the pump starts, the float is stuck or tangled
  • !Check the discharge pipe outside for a blockage, ice or a crushed section

The Ramapo River and your basement

Water tables in the Ramapo Valley rise with heavy rain, and the river itself has a history of flooding. The National Weather Service flood stage at the Mahwah gauge is 8 feet, and the highest recorded crest in its historical list is 15.77 feet in August 2011, nearly double flood stage.

Not every Mahwah home is near the river, but groundwater moves through the valley and a high water table pushes into basements well away from the bank. A working pump with a backup is the first line of defense in those homes.

Choosing and installing a pump

Submersible

Sits in the pit, quiet and effective. The common choice for basements.

Pedestal

Motor above the pit, easier to service and usually less powerful.

Battery backup

A second pump and a deep-cycle battery for power outages. Many storm failures are outages.

Water-powered backup

Uses water pressure to move groundwater and needs no battery, subject to local rules and water use.

A good installation includes a clean, level pit, a check valve on the discharge, a discharge pipe that carries water away from the house and a GFCI outlet. Discharge rules vary by municipality, and some prohibit sump water in the sanitary sewer, so ask the township where your discharge should go.

Maintenance that prevents emergencies

  • Test the pump twice a year by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching it start and stop.
  • Clear the pit of debris and check that the float moves freely.
  • Look at the discharge outlet outside after storms and in winter for ice or blockage.
  • Replace the backup battery on the manufacturer's schedule.
