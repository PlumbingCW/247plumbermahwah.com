Mahwah is the home base

Mahwah is a township in Bergen County with 25,487 residents at the 2020 census and an area of about 26 square miles, which makes it the largest municipality in the county by area. It includes Darlington, West Mahwah, Cragmere Park and Masonicus, and two ZIP codes, 07430 and 07495. Interstate 287, Route 17 and U.S. Route 202 all pass through it.

Our address is 1000 Wyckoff Ave, Mahwah, NJ 07430. Call the line from anywhere in the township and the same dispatch answers. Water in Mahwah comes from the township's wells and from Veolia, and the 2026 water quality report lists hardness from 91 to 203 ppm.

A residential-only section of Mahwah, laid out in 1909 around Oweno Lake, with streets such as Oweno, Airmount and Mahwah Roads.

Fardale

A section of Mahwah that was farmland into the 1960s and is now mostly single-family streets and condominium communities.

West Mahwah

A mostly residential section of Mahwah with some businesses and industry, reached along West Ramapo Avenue and the Valley Road corridor.

How the towns differ

Ramsey is the compact downtown borough to the south, with established streets, two NJ Transit stations and Route 17 along its edge. Oakland is downstream on the Ramapo River and Route 202, with wooded hills, parks and basements that feel the river valley's water table. Allendale is a small borough with homes from many eras and five municipal wells.

Because the towns differ, the calls differ. Ramsey calls often involve drains and water heaters in established homes. Oakland calls lean toward sump pumps and drainage. Allendale calls often start with the age of the pipes. Each town page explains the local reasons and links to the matching service.

Mahwah water, in brief

Plumbing standards are the same across all four towns, since New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Permit rules vary by town, so ask the local construction office.

The township's 2026 water quality report says about two thirds of Mahwah's water comes from seven wells along the Ramapo River valley and the rest from Veolia. Hardness runs from 91 to 203 ppm, which spans moderately hard to very hard. For hard water effects on heaters and fixtures, see water heater repair and faucet repair.
