When a failed water heater is an emergency

A tank that has rusted through can release dozens of gallons onto the floor. Even a slow leak at the base of a tank is a sign the steel shell has failed, and it tends to worsen without warning. Those are the situations where a same-day replacement matters more than shopping around for weeks.

Signs the tank itself has failed

  • !Water pooling under the unit that does not come from a fitting or the relief valve
  • !Rust-colored hot water together with a wet base
  • !A tank that has been repaired more than once in a short span
  • !A data plate that shows the unit is well past ten years old
  • !Hissing, steam or a metallic smell from the burner compartment

Call the 24/7 line at any hour. After-hours and weekend replacements are handled the same way as weekday jobs, and a plumber will confirm the unit type before arriving so the right heater is on the truck.

Replacing like for like is the quickest route. Changing fuel type or going tankless takes more planning because gas piping, venting and the electrical panel may need work.

Sizing for the household

A heater that is too small runs out mid-shower, and one that is too large wastes energy to keep unused water hot. Sizing is based on the number of people, the number of bathrooms and whether big fixtures such as a soaking tub run at the same time as the dishwasher. For tank units, the first-hour rating on the label matters more than tank volume alone.

What a replacement visit includes

  1. Confirm the failure and the fuel

    The plumber checks that the tank itself is the problem and notes the gas or electric supply, the vent path and the location.

  2. Shut down and drain

    The water, power or gas is shut off and the old tank is drained to a floor drain or outside.

  3. Set the new unit

    New flexible connectors, a new relief valve and a discharge pipe that runs to a safe point are standard.

  4. Vent, pipe and test

    Gas units get a leak test and a combustion check. Electric units get a circuit and element check.

  5. Expansion control and pressure

    On closed systems, an expansion tank is checked or added, and house water pressure is read.

  6. Remove the old heater

    The old unit leaves with the plumber, and the area is left clean.

Codes, permits and Mahwah water

New Jersey's plumbing subcode is based on the National Standard Plumbing Code. Permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office before any job that changes venting, gas piping or fuel type. A plumber can tell you what the work will need.

Mahwah draws about two thirds of its water from local wells, and the 2026 water report puts hardness between 91 and 203 ppm. In water like that, a new tank benefits from yearly flushing, and a tankless unit should be descaled on a schedule.

What affects the price of a water heater replacement

A like-for-like tank swap and a gas conversion to tankless are different projects. These factors explain the range, and the plumber should state the price before the old unit is touched.

Type and capacityTankless and hybrid units cost more than a standard tank.
Fuel and ventingChanging from electric to gas, or relining a flue, adds work.
Code itemsExpansion tank, relief discharge, seismic or pan requirements and permits add parts or time.
Access and removalTight spaces and stairs slow the job.
Time of the callAfter-hours work can carry a different rate, stated up front.

Homeowners policies typically cover sudden water damage from a failed heater but not the cost of the heater. A slow leak that went unnoticed may be excluded, so keep photos and the old data plate if you file.
