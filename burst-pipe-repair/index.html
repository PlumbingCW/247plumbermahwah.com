When a burst pipe is an emergency

Every burst pipe is an emergency, because the damage grows by the minute. Water soaks insulation and drywall, runs through ceilings and finds electrical fixtures. The steps in the first few minutes matter more than the repair method.

The first ten minutes

  • !Shut off the main water valve. Know where it is before you need it
  • !Open the lowest faucet in the house and flush a toilet to drain the lines
  • !Turn off the electricity to flooded rooms at the breaker, if the panel is dry and reachable
  • !Move valuables and furniture to higher ground and use towels or a wet vacuum on the surface
  • !Photograph the damage and the pipe before cleanup, then call

Frozen pipes and thawing

Mahwah winters bring freezing nights, and pipes in exterior walls and unheated spaces are at risk. A frozen pipe does not always burst when it freezes. It often bursts when it thaws and pressure returns. If a faucet gives no water in cold weather, a pipe may be frozen.

  • Leave the faucet open so the thawing water can flow out.
  • Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet toward the frozen section.
  • Never use an open flame or a torch. It starts fires and can burst a pipe.
  • If you cannot find the freeze or the pipe is behind a wall, call a plumber and shut off the main.

How a burst pipe repair works

  1. Isolate and drain

    The plumber confirms the main is off, drains the system and finds the break.

  2. Cut out the damaged section

    A burst copper pipe typically splits along a seam. The damaged length is cut back to sound pipe.

  3. Replace the section

    New pipe and fittings, copper, PEX or another approved material, are installed with the right connectors.

  4. Pressure test

    The line is pressurized and checked for leaks before anything is closed up.

  5. Check what else may fail

    A pipe that froze once may have thinned others nearby, and the plumber looks at the rest of the run.

  6. Dry-out guidance

    Wet drywall and insulation need to dry quickly. We advise on what to remove and when a restoration company should step in.

Mahwah homes and burst pipes

Houses in Mahwah range from older homes in Cragmere Park and Darlington to newer subdivisions along Route 17, and each has its own risks. Older homes may have long runs of galvanized or copper that have thinned with age. New additions and finished basements often put pipes in exterior walls with little insulation.

Garages, outbuildings and homes on exposed wooded lots deserve extra attention in winter, since pipes there have the least protection. Foam sleeves and heat tape are inexpensive compared with a flooded room.
