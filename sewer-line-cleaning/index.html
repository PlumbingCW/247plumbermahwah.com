Main line backup: the emergency

A blocked main sewer line sends water to the lowest opening in the house, which is usually a basement floor drain, a shower or a tub. By the time that happens, the line has been slowing for days or weeks. The longer the water sits, the greater the damage and the cleanup.

What to do while you wait

  • !Do not flush or run water, including the washing machine and dishwasher
  • !Locate the main cleanout, a capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement, so the plumber can reach it fast
  • !Keep floors clear and move belongings from the affected area
  • !Do not open the cleanout cap yourself if sewage is rising behind it
  • !Call the 24/7 line and describe the fixtures that are affected

Calls are taken at any hour, and after-hours cleaning is scheduled the same way as daytime work. If a same-day visit is not possible, we tell you so and help you limit damage in the meantime.

The cleaning process

  1. Open the cleanout

    Where there is one, the main cleanout gives direct access to the line. If there is none, the toilet flange or a vent is the alternative.

  2. Cut through the blockage

    A large cable machine with a root cutter or a blade head opens the line and cuts roots to the pipe wall.

  3. Jet if needed

    A hydro jet follows to scour the debris away. Read more about hydro jetting.

  4. Camera the line

    A camera pass confirms the line is clear and shows any damage, so you know whether the problem will return.

  5. Review and plan

    You see the findings, and any repair or maintenance schedule is discussed before anything else is done.

Why Mahwah lines clog

Mahwah is the largest municipality in Bergen County by area, with many wooded properties and long runs between the house and the street. Roots find their way into laterals through joints in older pipe, especially clay and cast iron. Hardness in Mahwah water, 91 to 203 ppm in the 2026 report, adds mineral scale on top of grease and waste layers inside the pipe.

If your home connects to the township sewer, the Department of Public Works Water and Sewer office handles the main in the street. Your cleaning covers the house-side line. If your property uses a septic system, we do not pump the tank.

Cleaning on a schedule

  • Lines with a history of root intrusion often need cleaning once a year or every other year.
  • Restaurants and commercial kitchens benefit from grease line cleaning on a regular schedule.
  • After a cleaning, a camera pass records the condition, and a second pass a year later shows how fast buildup returns.
  • If cleaning is needed more than twice a year, the pipe probably needs repair. See sewer line repair.

Codes and standards that apply

Sewer cleaning does not usually need a permit, but any repair that follows does fall under the plumbing code. New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. If roots return within a year, ask for a camera inspection and not another cleaning.
