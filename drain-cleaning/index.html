When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow bathroom sink can wait until morning. A backup that reaches several fixtures at once cannot. When the toilet gurgles, the shower fills with gray water and the basement floor drain bubbles, the blockage is almost always in the main line, and wastewater has nowhere to go but back into the house.

Call right away if you see any of these

  • !Water or sewage rising in a tub, shower or floor drain when you flush or run the washer
  • !Two or more fixtures backing up together
  • !Standing water in the basement with a sewer smell
  • !Overflow reaching finished flooring, a furnace or electrical outlets
  • !A kitchen sink that fills and will not drain after plunging

Our dispatch line answers at any hour, and after-hours and weekend visits are scheduled the same way as daytime ones. If water is near electrical equipment, stay clear of it until the power to that area is off.

What causes clogs in Mahwah homes

Most clogs are ordinary. Kitchen lines collect cooking grease that cools and hardens inside the pipe. Bathroom lines collect hair, soap film and the scale that hard water leaves behind. Mahwah's 2026 water quality report lists hardness between 91 and 203 ppm, which runs from moderately hard to very hard on the USGS scale, so soap scum and mineral buildup narrow drain lines faster than they would with soft water.

Roots are the other local cause. Lots near the Ramapo Valley are wooded, and tree roots find their way into older sewer laterals at the joints. A line that clogs every few months in the same place is usually telling you about a joint, a sag or a root, not about what went down the drain.

How a plumber finds and clears the blockage

  1. Find out which fixtures are affected

    The pattern matters more than the single sink. One fixture points to a branch. Several fixtures on different floors point to the main line.

  2. Start at the closest access

    That is usually the fixture trap or a cleanout, the capped pipe stub in the basement or yard. Opening the cleanout also shows whether the main line is full of water.

  3. Cable the line

    A drain machine feeds a spinning cable with a cutter head into the pipe. Different heads cut grease, hair or roots, and the head chosen depends on what the first pass brings back.

  4. Camera the line when it keeps coming back

    A waterproof drain camera shows the pipe wall, any offset joints and the point where debris collects. Clearing a clog without seeing the pipe is a guess, and the same blockage often returns.

  5. Jet when scale or grease needs more than a cable

    A hydro jet scours the pipe wall with pressurized water. It is the better tool for heavy grease and scale, as long as the pipe is sound.

  6. Test the flow

    Water runs through the fixtures and the line is checked before the job is closed. A drain that only drains slowly is not finished.

Codes and standards that apply

New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and that code sets standards for drain sizing, traps and venting. A drain that is cleared but still slow can point to a venting or slope problem, which is a repair and not a cleaning. Permit rules vary, so ask the local construction office before any change to the piping itself.

Keeping drains clear between visits

  • Wipe grease from pans into the trash, not the sink. A strainer in the kitchen and in each shower catches most of the rest.
  • Flush toilet paper only. Wipes labeled flushable still collect in the line.
  • Run hot tap water for a minute after washing dishes so grease moves past the first bends.
  • Locate your main cleanout before you need it. Knowing where it is saves time on an emergency call.
